Macro Signal

Coordination infrastructure centralizes when participants can't credibly exit under stress. PoS networks lack price anchors because staking ties up the exit option itself—validators harvest volatility but can't stabilize it without external collateral. Electricity demand won't clear stress either; ninety years of tech haven't made it price-responsive because physical systems can't coordinate fast enough when supply tightens.

AI compute concentrates where energy and capital are cheapest to coordinate at scale, which means dollar-denominated infrastructure. The through-line: systems that look decentralized during calm falsely advertise their resilience. Watch stablecoin composition during the next ETH drawdown—if USDC dominance against decentralized alternatives spikes above 85%, coordination is already breaking toward fiat rails before price confirms it.

Proof-of-Stake Dynamics: The Elusive Price Anchor and Endogenous Volatility Harvesting
Mikhail Perepelitsa  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Academic paper develops first open-economy macro model of Proof-of-Stake networks, proving a unique equilibrium exists but with ~46-year relaxation half-life for Ethereum—meaning token prices can remain displaced from fundamental value for decades due to extreme macroeconomic inertia. Models how passive vs. active speculation interacts with staking dynamics to create endogenous volatility, framing PoS tokens as systems with fundamentally different price discovery than commodities or equities. Provides formal mathematical structure explaining why ETH price behavior post-Merge defies traditional asset frameworks.
If relaxation timescales are this long, PoS networks function more like sovereign currencies with structurally indeterminate prices than commodities with supply anchors. Watch staking ratio divergence from model predictions as signal of capital regime shift; if active speculation dominates passive staking, volatility becomes self-reinforcing regardless of adoption fundamentals.

Electricity demand has not become more price-responsive despite ninety years of technological change
Peter Kudela, Tomas Havranek, Zuzana Irsova  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Meta-analysis of 4,720 elasticity estimates (1934-2024) shows electricity demand is structurally inelastic and has NOT become more price-responsive despite 90 years of tech change (smart meters, storage, automation). Best-identified studies find short-run elasticity of -0.09 (statistically indistinguishable from zero); long-run only -0.38. This falsifies the central planning assumption embedded in IRA, EU Green Deal, and utility rate design: that price signals alone can manage demand curves during intermittent renewable buildout. If demand won't flex, either you overbuild dispatchable capacity (gas/nuclear) or you ration directly—price mechanism fails as the coordination layer.
If this holds, watch for: (1) renewed nuclear/gas buildout masked as 'reliability' after 2027 grid stress events, (2) political shift from carbon pricing to direct consumption mandates, (3) distributed storage subsidies rising as implicit admission that demand-side management via price failed. Counterfactual: real-time pricing pilots in TX/CA show sustained demand destruction above -0.3 elasticity—but sample selection bias may explain (early adopters ≠ median household).

If this holds, watch foreign AI development funding flows—countries building sovereign compute will need dollar swap lines or burn FX reserves. Counterfactual: China's domestic GPU substitution timeline vs. their AI ambition gap.

Algorithmic Intermediation and the International Transmission of U.S. Monetary Policy
Fernando Toledo, Luis Dimotta Bré, Gabriel Montes-Rojas  ·  arXiv ·  2026
This paper isolates a structural shift in how U.S. monetary policy propagates to emerging markets: algorithmic fund management amplifies capital flow volatility not through speed or size, but through model homogeneity. When algos across funds converge on similar signals, their correlated errors reinforce each other during stress, intensifying outflows after Fed shocks. Diverse models stabilize flows by offsetting errors. Evidence from 2000–2024 shows herding effects emerge only in high-volatility regimes. The finding reframes regulatory focus: preserve model diversity, not fund size limits. For Malcolm, this is the transmission channel governing the next EM crisis—and a directional indicator for where capital reallocates when correlation spikes.
If algorithmic model convergence continues (watch fund strategy disclosures, patent clustering), EM volatility will spike asymmetrically during the next Fed tightening cycle. Counterindicator: sustained dispersion in quant factor loadings across top 20 systematic funds would signal stabilization. Position accordingly in EM equity volatility instruments.

If compute financing markets develop real secondary liquidity (tokenized capacity rights, compute REITs, usage futures), watch for: (1) datacenter capex accelerating 2-3x beyond current hyperscaler budgets, (2) private credit funds rotating from real estate into infrastructure debt, (3) retail access via structured products. Counterfactual: if financing stays traditional, AI buildout remains constrained to balance-sheet-heavy incumbents and sovereign wealth.

Messy Research, Certification and the Monetization of Science
Johan Fourie  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Models why scientific credentialing shifts from reputation-based (free) to price-based when AI makes polish cheaper than quality verification. Core mechanism: when LLMs can produce plausible manuscripts faster than peer review can evaluate underlying rigor, polish stops functioning as a quality signal. Entry floods. Certification value rises to fill the reputational gap. The tolerant journal (reviews some, accepts unread overflow) premium peaks then falls; the strict journal (rejects unread pile) premium can end higher than baseline if quality becomes scarce relative to review capacity. Crosses when high-quality work drops below ~50% of submissions. Directly applicable beyond academia: any credential market where AI lowers production cost asymmetrically versus verification cost will shift from reputation to explicit certification pricing.
If this holds, watch credential inflation across professional certifications (CFA, actuarial, law bar prep). Counterfactual: if AI verification tools catch up to generation tools within 18 months, certification premium should compress rather than expand. Track ratio of submission volume to editor capacity at Nature/Science as leading indicator.

When Shippers Become Algorithms: Candidate Exposure, Information Design, and the Concentration of LLM-Mediated Freight Markets
Takahiro Ezaki, Naoto Imura, Katsuhiro Nishinari  ·  arXiv ·  2026
When commercial LLMs (GPT, Claude, Gemini) autonomously select freight carriers in a simulated matching market, they collapse onto the same vendor immediately: 76% of requests went to one carrier on day one despite agents drawing from different candidate pools. The mechanism is convergent prompt reasoning toward perceived 'safe' options under uncertainty, creating Schelling points without explicit coordination. The countermeasure that works: platforms control the candidate exposure set (what the LLM sees) not the ranking algorithm. This is the first known study showing agent-mediated B2B markets structurally concentrate faster than human-mediated ones, with profound implications for platform design, antitrust enforcement, and any sector where procurement becomes delegated to model inference.
First empirical demonstration of how LLM agent delegation creates Schelling-point market concentration in B2B platforms—a mechanism that will generalize beyond freight as agentic procurement spreads.
If LLM-mediated procurement scales, watch for rapid vendor concentration in markets with opacity (healthcare equipment, industrial supplies, SaaS tooling). The counterfactual: platforms that randomize or constrain candidate exposure should see sustained diversity. Regulatory bodies treating this as algorithmic collusion will misdiagnose the mechanism—it's emergent inference convergence, not coordination.
