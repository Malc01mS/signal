Macro Signal

Central banks encode policy through forward guidance because market participants cannot independently verify the transmission mechanism in real time. When uncertainty about the policy rule itself rises, this coordination infrastructure fails—traders revert to positioning for discrete regime breaks rather than marginal adjustments, and liquidity concentrates in benchmark maturities while fragmentation accelerates everywhere else.

The FEDS paper documents how elevated uncertainty forces the Fed to communicate more while achieving less, a direct symptom of coordination breakdown. When guidance loses credibility as an anchor, volatility clustering moves from the tails into core rates markets, and dealer balance sheets withdraw precisely when intermediation matters most.

Watch the spread between on-the-run and off-the-run Treasury volatility. Widening confirms coordination is breaking and the curve will trade in jumps, not slides. Position for convexity in short-dated options over linear duration.

FEDS Paper: Risks and Uncertainty in Monetary Policy
FRB: Working Papers · 
Federal Reserve working paper by Adrian/Giannone/Luciani/West unifies two historically separate risk assessment frameworks—scenario analysis (narratives without probabilities) and distributional forecasting (probabilities without economic stories)—into a single conditional density framework called Scenario Synthesis. This gives central banks a formal method to weight multiple narrative scenarios against a baseline distribution, making tail risk assessment mathematically coherent rather than ad-hoc. For Malcolm: this is the plumbing update that determines how the Fed will frame policy risk going forward, which shapes forward guidance credibility and option-implied volatility structure in rate markets.
If Fed adopts this framework operationally, watch FOMC minutes for explicit scenario weighting language and probability assignments to tail outcomes. Counterfactual: continued reliance on point forecasts with detached qualitative risk paragraphs signals internal resistance to distributional transparency, keeping vol regimes choppy.

Competitive Market Behavior of LLMs
Pawel Struski, Jakub Swistak, Inez Okulska  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Researchers replicated classic double-auction experiments but replaced human traders with LLM agents from multiple model families. Markets populated by LLMs showed degraded price discovery—slower or absent convergence toward equilibrium compared to human baselines, indicating less efficient resource allocation. Agent behavior exhibited high heterogeneity across models and assigned roles (buyer/seller), suggesting LLMs lack the behavioral consistency that market mechanisms assume. This surfaces a structural misalignment: LLMs aren't drop-in replacements for economic actors even when they pass reasoning benchmarks, because markets rely on predictable incentive responses that current architectures don't replicate.
If this holds, deploying LLM agents as autonomous market participants (algorithmic trading, supplier negotiations, DeFi protocols) risks market instability or persistent mispricing. Watch: regulatory scrutiny of AI trading agents, exchange circuit-breaker frequency in crypto markets with high bot penetration, and whether next-gen models (o3, Claude Opus 4) show convergent behavior in adversarial auction settings as a capabilities benchmark.

America’s Naval Disaster
Project Syndicate · 
US shipbuilding capacity has collapsed relative to China—not due to lack of subsidies, but structural barriers: Jones Act protectionism kills competition, defense contracting ossification prevents new entrants, and labor/regulatory overhead makes yards 3-5x more expensive than Asian counterparts. Trump's push for domestic naval expansion ignores these root causes and risks pouring capital into a system that cannot scale without fundamental regulatory surgery. China produces 50%+ of global tonnage; US produces <0.5%. The gap isn't reversible through tariffs or procurement mandates without addressing why yards can't compete on cost or speed even with subsidies already in place.
If naval budgets rise without Jones Act reform or defense contractor competition reform, watch for cost overruns that mirror F-35 program dynamics—money flows, hulls don't materialize. Counterfactual: if Congress moves to open shipbuilding to foreign yards or exempt military builds from Jones Act, equity in Asian shipbuilders (Korea, Japan) becomes leveraged play on US naval orders.

Tariff Threats, Macroeconomic Expectations, and Policy Communication Strategies: Experiments Based on a Multi-Agent System
Jianhao Lin, Lexuan Sun, Yixin Yan  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Researchers built LLM agents calibrated to 300 Michigan Survey households and exposed them to tariff threat language over simulated months. They replicated distributional patterns from actual Liberation Day tariff surveys, then ran counterfactual experiments showing how immediacy, semantic progression, and sender identity shape inflation/unemployment expectations and their dispersion. Critically, the mechanism traces through attention allocation, ambiguity processing, and causal narrative formation—not just information content. Central bank communication can coordinate beliefs, but effects depend on message framing, not just credibility.
Trump's tariff threats are live policy instruments right now—this offers a transmission mechanism for how language choices move expectations before enactment.
If policy language drives expectations through narrative structure rather than factual content, watch for central banks shifting toward pre-commitment framing and away from data-dependent language during uncertainty regimes. Counterfactual: if dispersion increases despite messaging clarity, credibility erosion is the binding constraint.
