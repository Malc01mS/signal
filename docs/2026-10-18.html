Macro Signal

Fiscal uncertainty forces price-setters to defer adjustment, creating clustered repricing events when clarity arrives. During this suspension, the system routes flow into assets that require no coordination to hold—physically settled stores of value that bypass counterparty chains.

Gold exports surging while debt relief mechanisms concentrate borrower information in fewer intermediaries shows the same pattern: coordination infrastructure either atomizes into non-systemic bilaterals or collapses into monopolistic nodes. The FEDS paper documents the delay mechanism; the other two show where flow goes during the wait and what survives after. Watch sovereign debt restructuring timelines extending while gold lease rates compress—that spread measures how long the system can hold its breath before forced coordination.

FEDS Paper: Inattentive Price Setting and Inflation Under Fiscal Policy Uncertainty
FRB: Working Papers · 
Fed researchers model how firms decide what information to gather before setting prices when facing both cost and fiscal policy uncertainty. At high public debt levels, firms face a U-shaped attention curve: low debt means taxes don't matter much; extremely high debt makes taxes so volatile that firms allocate more attention to fiscal signals. This creates a counterintuitive result—inflation volatility can increase non-linearly as debt crosses certain thresholds, validated with euro-area micro data. For Malcolm: this challenges the standard 'debt always equals inflation' narrative with a structural mechanism showing debt can destabilize inflation *through firm-level information processing*, not just money supply.
If U.S. debt continues climbing past 130% GDP with no fiscal anchor, watch for inflation expectations surveys showing increased firm-level uncertainty dispersion and nominal volatility rising even without actual fiscal expansion. Counterfactual: credible fiscal consolidation should compress inflation vol even at high debt levels by reducing policy uncertainty.

Gold is now the top U.S. export
Hacker News · 
Gold became the United States' largest export category, overtaking traditional leaders like semiconductors, petroleum, and aircraft. This is a structural indicator of capital flow reversal: foreign central banks (especially BRICS+) are converting dollar reserves into physical gold held in U.S. vaults, then physically removing it. It validates Lyn Alden's thesis that we're in the late stage of the long-term debt cycle where hard assets displace financial claims. The mechanism: as Treasury yields stay elevated and geopolitical blocs formalize, gold becomes the only universally accepted reserve asset without counterparty risk—driving unprecedented physical demand that now registers as a top-line export.
If this persists, watch U.S. gold reserves (currently ~8,100 tonnes) for any official acknowledgment of drawdown or re-appraisal. Counterfactual: reversal only if real rates sustain deeply positive (>2%) and dollar strengthens structurally vs. gold—unlikely given fiscal trajectory.

The Financing of Debt Relief: Intermediary Liquidity and the Value of Borrower Information
Arka P. Bandyopadhyay  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Uses 1.9M Ginnie Mae loan repurchase events (2019–2022) to reveal nonbank servicers as structurally fragile during liquidity freezes. When funding dried up in March 2020, nonbanks delayed $19B in loan repurchases versus depositories in identical pools, rationing cash toward smaller balances and abandoning borrowers who most needed modification. The mechanism: nonbanks lack deposit funding and must ration finite warehouse lines by *information value per dollar of cash deployed*. This confirms Alden-style structural vulnerability: credit markets now depend on shadow intermediaries that fail precisely when borrowers need relief, amplifying procyclicality.
If nonbank mortgage share keeps rising (now ~55% of servicing), watch warehouse lending spreads and forbearance uptake divergence in the next downturn—nonbanks will ration modification capital first, forcing GSE or Fed intervention.

Displacement Is Endogenous: AI Workers in a Search-and-Matching Labor Market
Ibrahim Denis Fofanah  ·  arXiv ·  2026
This paper models AI as literal job applicants in a frictionful labor market and finds displacement mechanism inverts at different AI wage levels. When AI costs 70% of human wage, firms expand output (cheap inputs) so displacement is modest (4.1pp unemployment) and layoffs dominate. At wage *parity*, no layoffs occur but hiring freezes—unemployment rises to 9.2pp, entirely through blocked entry. The result: price gap determines *which workers* lose (incumbents vs. entrants) and product demand elasticity governs total displacement scale. This is the first formalization showing AI labor substitution isn't monotonic—it's a phase transition across cost regimes.
First search-and-matching model showing AI displacement operates through *layoff vs. hiring* margins depending on wage gap—arrives as policy debates fixate on job loss without micro-level labor market structure.
If AI API pricing converges toward human-equivalent cost (not undercutting), watch youth/new-grad unemployment spike while layoff rates stay flat—hiring freezes precede firing waves. Counterpoint: if compute costs crater and AI stays structurally cheaper, incumbent churn accelerates but total employment may stabilize via output expansion.
