Macro Signal

Treasury market liquidity depends on a narrow set of dealers absorbing auction flow and warehousing duration risk. When these intermediaries pull back—due to balance sheet constraints or capital charges—the clearing price becomes volatile and the bid-ask spread widens non-linearly.

The IFDP paper quantifies how supply shocks transmit through this bottleneck. Dealer capacity hasn't scaled with issuance, meaning each incremental Treasury auction hits thinner absorption. This creates a coordination problem: primary dealers must simultaneously position for customer flow and manage their own inventory limits, and neither constraint adjusts quickly.

Watch repo rates around quarter-ends and auction cycles. If overnight general collateral spreads to IORB widen beyond 10bp consistently, it signals dealers are at their regulatory limits and can't intermediate smoothly. That's when convexity hedging or foreign central bank sales trigger disorderly moves.

IFDP Paper: Estimating Yield Impacts of Treasury Demand and Supply Changes
FRB: Working Papers · 
Federal Reserve researchers model how Treasury demand elasticity has changed over time as buyer composition shifted: foreign official holders (less price-sensitive) declined while hedge funds and private investors (more price-sensitive) rose. Result: markets now move more per unit of supply. A $100bn supply increase now lifts 5yr yields ~3bps, but the elasticity itself is regime-dependent. This means duration absorption capacity is lower than historical averages suggest, and volatility should rise structurally as issuance grows. The framework isolates demand shocks from supply shocks using IV—rare methodological rigor for Treasury market structure.
If foreign official holdings continue declining (especially China/Japan unwinding), each Treasury auction should produce larger yield moves than 2010s average. Watch basis trade unwinds or hedge fund deleveraging as amplification events. Counterfactual: Fed restarting QE would overwhelm this effect by replacing price-sensitive with price-insensitive demand.

AI for AI: Optimizing Additional Infrastructure Build-out to Power Artificial Intelligence Data Centers
Alexander Crosier, Kyle Onghai, Ronnie Sircar  ·  arXiv ·  2026
This paper models AI datacenter electricity demand and grid supply expansion as coupled stochastic processes rather than deterministic forecasts, showing how mismatched growth rates between compute demand and generation capacity create structural price volatility. The framework treats generation build-out as a stochastic control problem where investors optimize timing/intensity of new supply under demand uncertainty. Key insight: demand surges don't automatically trigger proportional supply responses due to financing lags, regulatory friction, and investor risk—creating extended periods where power becomes the binding constraint on AI scaling, not capital or chips.
If this thesis holds, power purchase agreements become the new talent/chip war—watch for hyperscalers vertically integrating into generation (SMRs, dedicated gas plants) rather than waiting for utility responses. Counterfactual: if AI workload efficiency gains (better models per watt) outpace datacenter expansion, demand peaks sooner than supply can overshoot.

The Political Earthquake From Germany’s China Shock
Project Syndicate · 
Germany's social compact depended on export-led manufacturing translating into broad prosperity and institutional legitimacy. China's manufacturing ascent hollowed out mid-tier German production (not just low-end), concentrating gains in capital-intensive sectors while employment collapsed in trade-exposed regions—the exact geographies where AfD now surges. This mirrors the U.S. China Shock thesis but breaks the European exceptionalism narrative: even coordinated market economies with strong unions can't hold the center when the industrial base erodes. The implication is structural: Europe's political stability assumed persistent manufacturing rents that no longer exist.
If German manufacturing employment continues contracting in non-automotive sectors, watch for SPD/Green vote share collapse in Rust Belt equivalents (Saxony, Thuringia) and further AfD normalization as the only party naming the mechanism. Counterfactual: if Germany redirects industrial policy toward friend-shoring/re-industrialization with actual job creation (not just subsidies), political fragmentation may stabilize.

Tradeable Import Certificates for Strategic Supply Security
Sebastian Kranz  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Proposes tradeable import certificates (TIC) as a market mechanism to enforce domestic production quotas and supply security targets while maintaining trade efficiency—countries issue certificates for imports, firms must hold them to import, creating a price signal that decentralizes enforcement. Unlike tariffs/subsidies, TICs make supply security targets explicit and robust against treaty violations or coercion, with prices adjusting endogenously as geopolitical reliability shifts. The paper microfounds this around economic coercion dynamics: countries set targets based on shortage salience (semiconductors, rare earths), bilateral dependence (China-U.S.), and norms against yielding. Suggests quantity-based security frameworks could replace tariff bargaining as the focal point for future trade architecture.
Trump's tariff threats, EU's Critical Raw Materials Act, and CHIPS Act implementation create live policy landscape where this mechanism could shift from theory to practice.
If post-Trump trade policy moves toward explicit supply security mandates (not just tariffs), watch for TIC-like mechanisms in semiconductor/battery/pharma agreements. Counterfactual: continued tariff escalation without quantity frameworks would signal reversion to 20th-century trade war logic rather than strategic autarky design.

Redistributive Policies for the Times of Transformative AI
Polish/German economists model wealth concentration dynamics under transformative AI scenarios, arguing that without redistributive intervention, automation-driven productivity gains will accrue almost exclusively to compute/robot owners, creating inequality exceeding industrial-era peaks. They compare policy mechanisms (UBI, UBC, compute permits, capital taxes) and conclude that only rent-sharing instruments—specifically Universal Basic Capital or capital-tax-funded UBI—can durably counteract ownership concentration when labor's marginal product approaches zero. The framework assumes aligned AI but shows that alignment alone doesn't solve the distributional problem; ownership structure does.
If TAI compresses inference costs while concentrating ownership (current trajectory), watch for: (1) accelerating proposals for compute taxation or equity distribution schemes in AI policy circles, (2) sovereign wealth funds pivoting toward direct stake-taking in frontier labs, (3) UBI pilot expansions tied explicitly to AI productivity dividends rather than social safety nets. Counterfactual: if open-source inference tools proliferate faster than ownership consolidation, rent-capture becomes impractical and the whole redistribution thesis weakens.

A prelude to the theory of Real-World Asset (RWA) Tokenization
Tang constructs the first rigorous economic framework for RWA tokenization, distinguishing standard RWAs, stablecoins, and Digital Asset Treasuries (DATs) by the role of underlying assets. Core mechanism: continuous secondary-market trading generates information recovery, reducing adverse-selection costs over time—this endogenously affects both issuance pricing and optimal token design. The DAT model formalizes how market valuation, capital raising, and crypto accumulation interact dynamically, with Strategy as the empirical anchor. This names what Malcolm has observed empirically (MicroStrategy's BTC treasury strategy, Tether's T-bill positioning) but now provides the mechanism explaining why these structures work and under what conditions they optimize.
If DAT theory holds, watch for: (1) explicit adverse-selection pricing in token launches that reference secondary liquidity depth, (2) corporate issuance strategies tied to crypto accumulation cycles rather than pure equity dilution logic, (3) regulatory clarity treating DATs as distinct from standard securities. Counterfactual: DATs collapse into standard equity if crypto volatility destroys the treasury-premium mechanism.
