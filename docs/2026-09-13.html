The Organization That Remade India
Palladium · 
Palladium dissects how the RSS (Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh) executed a century-long ground-up transformation of Indian society through hyperlocal organizational discipline, not parliamentary maneuvering. The piece maps their operational mechanics: daily shakhas (local cells), lifetime cadre development, parallel institutional infrastructure (education, labor, media), and patient ideological colonization of civil society before capturing the state. This is realpolitik infrastructure-building at civilizational timescale—relevant as Malcolm tracks how power actually accrues in multipoint systems (cf. CCP's organizational Leninism, tech platform moats). The RSS case study inverts the typical "elections → policy → culture" assumption.
India crosses 20% of global GDP (PPP) trajectory as Modi's third term accelerates institutional embedding—understanding RSS's century-long structural playbook illuminates how civilizational projects actually consolidate power.
If organizational substrate precedes political capture (RSS thesis), watch for non-state institution-building in fragmenting Western systems—homeschool networks, parallel payment rails, local governance experiments. Counterfactual: if top-down state power still dominates, these stay marginal. India's next decade tests whether the RSS model survives prosperity or was only effective during scarcity.
