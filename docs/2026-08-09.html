Macro Signal

Interest rate volatility forces insurers to shorten duration, concentrating liquidity demand at the front end while draining stable long-term capital. Currency unions without fiscal transfers collapse inward during stress, as members compete for scarce risk-sharing capacity that doesn't exist at scale.

All three picks show coordination infrastructure that worked under stability now operating in reverse. AI may predict exchange rates better when volatility rises, but that just means more capital chasing fewer liquid hedges. Insurers shift from patient capital to redemption risk. Currency blocs reveal they never built the shock absorbers they assumed existed. Watch sovereign-corporate basis spreads in euro periphery credit. When insurers pull duration and cross-border risk sharing freezes, that basis should blow out before rates themselves signal distress.

AI and Exchange Rate Predictability
Amin Izadyar  ·  arXiv ·  2026
This paper uses ChatGPT and DeepSeek to forecast currency returns from economic fundamentals, achieving 0.7+ Sharpe ratio on a long-short strategy—effectively solving the exchange rate disconnect puzzle that has stumped econometricians since 1983. The result suggests LLMs extract systematic signal from macro releases that traditional models miss, likely via implicit Taylor rule reasoning. If replicable, this represents a structural break: fundamentals become tradeable again, macro data releases regain alpha generation capacity, and sovereign bond/FX correlation structure shifts. Malcolm should watch: (1) whether hedge funds publishing AI-FX strategies compress this alpha within 6-12 months, (2) if central banks alter communication strategy knowing LLMs parse it better than humans, (3) whether this generalizes to other "efficient market" puzzles (equity risk premium, term structure).
If this holds, expect rapid proliferation of AI-driven macro strategies in 2025-26, tightening bid-ask spreads in FX majors, and potential arms race in central bank communication obfuscation. Watch for replication failures or published criticisms within 90 days—standard academic timeline—as the counterfactual.

From Long to Short: How Interest Rates Shape Life Insurance Markets
Ziang Li, Derek Wenning  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Shows how post-2008 variable annuity liabilities forced insurers into negative duration gaps, causing them to raise prices and withdraw from long-term life insurance issuance—resulting in a 31% GDP decline in long-term coverage by 2023. The mechanism: insurers can't perfectly hedge interest rate risk on existing books, so they distort NEW product offerings to offset portfolio duration mismatches. This is a financial plumbing story that connects Lyn Alden's debt-cycle framework to real economy outcomes: when rates move violently, institutions rationally pull back from long-duration commitments, which compounds fragility. The paper quantifies a specific transmission channel from monetary regime change to household balance sheet resilience.
If this mechanism generalizes, watch for continued retreat from long-duration private contracts (30-year mortgages, defined benefit pensions, infrastructure finance) as rate vol persists. Counterfactual: if Treasury vol (MOVE index) stabilizes sub-100 for 18+ months, insurers should begin re-entering long-term product issuance.

The Role of Risk Sharing in Attenuating Business Cycles Within Currency Unions
Alberto Pavia, Christian Proebsting  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Risk-sharing mechanisms (migration, fiscal transfers, diversified income streams, credit markets) don't just smooth consumption—they cut the local multiplier itself, stabilizing income volatility at the source. Using military buildups as natural experiments, authors find risk-sharing reduces state-level consumption volatility 4x, with half that benefit coming from income stabilization rather than direct consumption smoothing. This doubles the estimated integration benefit versus standard measures. Implication: currency unions without deep risk-sharing (Eurozone) face structurally amplified regional shocks because local multipliers compound rather than dampen. The U.S. federalist architecture isn't just insurance—it's a business cycle attenuator.
If risk-sharing depth drives multiplier dampening, watch for: (1) Eurozone periphery recessions deepening faster than U.S. state-level analogs despite similar initial shocks, (2) U.S. states with weaker interstate migration (regulatory capture, housing supply constraints) exhibiting higher consumption volatility than model predicts, (3) emerging crypto-dollarized economies lacking fiscal federalism showing boom-bust amplification even under hard money. Counterfactual: if China's internal migration system tightens further, provincial cycles should desynchronize and amplify.

The Rising Price of a Cheap Renminbi
Project Syndicate · 
The piece argues that keeping the renminbi cheap isn't just export support—it's a political commitment device that locks China into delaying consumption rebalancing. RMB appreciation would raise household purchasing power over imports and compress margins in export sectors, forcing either wage increases or sectoral reallocation. Beijing avoids this because it would accelerate the loss of industrial employment before services can absorb labor, exposing the unfinished transition. The longer they delay, the larger the eventual adjustment shock becomes—making currency policy a lever that reveals political economy constraints, not just trade strategy.
If China does allow RMB appreciation (watch CNY crossing 7.0 sustainably), it signals either: (1) confidence in domestic consumption transition, or (2) submission to external pressure with internal instability risk rising. Counterfactual: continued CNY weakness past 7.3 = doubling down on export-led path, raising probability of capital control tightening and financial repression deepening.

Digital State Capacity
Patrick Healy, Simon D. Angus, Paul Raschky  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Researchers construct a novel metric for state capacity by tracking government-held IPv4 address space as a proxy for deployable digital infrastructure across 150+ countries and 1,681 subnational regions (2019-2024). The thesis: IP holdings reveal which governments can actually implement policy digitally—not aspirational capability, but observable stock of networked systems. They validate it against corruption control and vaccination outcomes, showing digital infrastructure correlates with execution capacity. This operationalizes 'state capacity' debates by measuring the physical layer of governance—network addressability as institutional plumbing.
If digital state capacity determines policy execution velocity, governments with high IP/capita at Admin-1 level should outperform during infrastructure-dependent crises (pandemics, climate response). Watch IPv4 allocation shifts as leading indicator for which states can credibly deploy industrial policy AI tooling—China's provincial variance likely underreported.

The Scaling Paradox in Human-AI Collaboration
Anyan Qi, Mengxin Wang  ·  arXiv ·  2026
This analytical model proves that AI scaling laws—where bigger models = better performance—break down in human-AI systems when humans misperceive the AI's actual capability boundaries. Over-perception creates a *scaling paradox*: more capable AI paradoxically reduces joint system performance and firm profits because humans delegate incorrectly or fail to override when necessary. Under-perception wastes capability. The mechanism is calibration misalignment, not the AI itself. This reframes the AI deployment challenge from 'how capable is the model' to 'how accurately do operators understand its failure modes,' which has direct implications for enterprise AI ROI, liability design, and where capital should flow (interface/calibration tooling vs. raw model scale).
If this holds, watch for: (1) enterprise AI contracts shifting from accuracy SLAs to *calibration accuracy* SLAs; (2) startup focus on 'AI confidence communication layers' rather than frontier model training; (3) regulatory frameworks mandating capability boundary transparency. Counterfactual: if scaling continues to drive adoption despite paradox, human learning/adaptation is faster than this model assumes.

Preying on Leveraged ETFs
Yinhong Zhao  ·  arXiv ·  2026
Arbitrageurs exploited Korean leveraged ETFs' mandated daily rebalancing at market close, buying ahead of the predictable flows and selling into the artificially enlarged move they created. The mechanism added 47% annualized volatility to Samsung/SK Hynix in 2026 and extracted ₩4 trillion from retail holders in eight weeks. The paper proves pre-open U.S. news reversed the next day only after these products listed, demonstrating a pure manufactured swing. Korea's fix—dispersing rebalances intraday—failed because the reference price still sized the order; switching to a two-print average halved exploitability.
First academic documentation of a microstructure exploit that manufactured systemic volatility and transferred $3B+ in wealth via predictable forced flows—a live example of Alden-style structural fragility in levered passive vehicles.
If leveraged passive vehicles grow as vol-targeting becomes consensus retail strategy, watch for similar microstructure parasitism in illiquid closes (crypto perpetuals, single-stock levered ETFs). Counter: if regulators mandate randomized or VWAP-based rebalances, the exploit collapses but doesn't eliminate the underlying fragility of path-dependent products in thin markets.
